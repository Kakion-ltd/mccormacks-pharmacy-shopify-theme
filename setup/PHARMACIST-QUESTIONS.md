# Questions before purchase: a draft for the pharmacist

> **This is our suggestion for you to correct, not a regulatory determination.**
> It was put together by the web team from each product's SmPC, the HPRA
> register and PSI guidance. You decide which medicines need questions, what
> the questions are, and which answers stop a sale. Cross out, add and reword
> freely; the website will follow what you send back.

Prepared 25 September 2026 for Fergal.

## How this fits with the website

Every medicine order on the website is designed to wait for a pharmacist to
review it before it is sent. That is the PSI requirement for internet supply
(section 2.5 of the PSI's guidance on internet supply), and it applies to all
licensed medicines, whether or not they have questions.

Questions are an extra step for a smaller set of products. For those, the
customer answers on the product page before they can add it to the bag. Any
answer marked **stop** below prevents the sale and tells the customer to speak
to a pharmacist or their GP. Any answer marked **review** lets the order through
to you with the answer highlighted. You see every answer on the order.

We looked at the 203 products on the website that hold an HPRA licence. We
suggest questions for **26 of them, in 8 sets**. Products in the same set share
the same questions.

   the other paracetamol products; tell us whether you want one limit across
   all of them.

   (PA22650/007/001, general sale), but it does not carry the mark, so its

## Set 1. Erectile dysfunction (5 products)

**Products:** Viagra Connect 50mg Tablets 4 Pack; Viagra Connect 50mg Tablets 8
Pack; Cialis For Men Tadalafil Tablets 4Pk; Cialis For Men Tadalafil Tablets
8Pk; Sidena 50mg Tablets 4 Pack.

**Licence conditions** (SmPCs PA23355/063/001, PA25208/001/001,
PA0711/170/002):

- Men aged 18 and over with erectile dysfunction. One tablet a day at most.
- Must not be used with nitrates or amyl nitrite ("poppers"), or riociguat.
  Sildenafil also not with ritonavir.
- Must not be used after a heart attack or stroke in the last 6 months (Cialis:
  heart attack in the last 90 days), with unstable angina, severe heart
  failure, or blood pressure below 90/50.
- Also excluded: severe liver disease, retinitis pigmentosa, sight lost in one
  eye from NAION.
- See a doctor first: breathlessness or chest pain on light exertion,
  alpha-blockers, severe kidney disease, sickle cell disease, myeloma,
  leukaemia, a bleeding disorder or an active stomach ulcer.
- **The HPRA register requires a consultation with a healthcare professional
  before non-prescription supply of Viagra Connect and Cialis.** Sidena's
  register entry has no such condition.

**Draft questions:**

1. Is this medicine for a man aged 18 or over who has difficulty getting or
   keeping an erection? *No: stop.*
2. Does he take any nitrate medicine (for example a GTN spray or
   isosorbide), or use "poppers" (amyl nitrite)? *Yes: stop.*
3. Does he take riociguat, or any medicine for HIV? *Yes: stop.*
4. In the last 6 months, has he had a heart attack, a stroke, unstable angina
   or severe heart failure? *Yes: stop.*
5. Does he get chest pain or become breathless with light exercise, such as
   climbing two flights of stairs? *Yes: stop.*
6. Has he been told he has low blood pressure, severe liver disease,
   retinitis pigmentosa, or sight loss in one eye caused by reduced blood flow
   to the eye? *Yes: stop.*
7. Does he take a medicine for blood pressure or the prostate, such as
   tamsulosin or doxazosin? *Yes: review.*
8. Does he have sickle cell disease, leukaemia, myeloma, a bleeding disorder,
   an active stomach ulcer, severe kidney disease, or a condition affecting
   the shape of the penis? *Yes: review.*

## Set 2. Codeine (1 product)

**Product:** Uniflu With Vitamin C Tablets 24Pk (codeine phosphate 10 mg with
paracetamol 500 mg, phenylephrine, diphenhydramine and caffeine).

> **This product is off the website until you decide.** PSI guidance says
> codeine medicines must not be accessible to the public for self-selection. A
> product a customer can add to a bag online is arguably exactly that, so it
> was taken off the online shop and left in the shops' tills. Please decide
> whether it should be sold online at all. If it should, the questions below
> are a starting point.

**Question for you: which other products contain codeine?** No product on the
website mentions codeine in its title or description, Uniflu included. We
found Uniflu only from its licence. So we cannot tell from the website which
products contain codeine, and only you can. Please list any others you stock
online, so they can be taken off the online shop in the same way until you
decide.

**Licence conditions** (SmPC PA1113/006/001):

- Age 12 and over. Ages 12 to 18: codeine 30 mg a day at most.
- **No longer than 3 days.**
- Must not be used: under 12; while breastfeeding; by a known CYP2D6
  ultra-rapid metaboliser; under 18 after tonsil or adenoid removal for sleep
  apnoea; with high blood pressure or heart disease, an overactive thyroid,
  glaucoma, difficulty passing urine or COPD; within 14 days of an MAOI.
- Dependence risk; no other paracetamol at the same time; anyone on regular
  medicines, especially antidepressants, should ask a doctor.

**What the PSI codeine guidance adds:** codeine is supplied only when the
pharmacist judges it the most appropriate treatment, with non-codeine
painkillers considered first; for no more than 3 days; with counselling on
dependence and overdose. **The guidance has no set list of questions for the
customer**; the questions below are ours, drawn from it.

**Draft questions:**

1. How old is the person who will take it? *Under 12: stop. 12 to 17: review.*
2. Have they already tried paracetamol or ibuprofen on its own for this,
   without enough relief? *No: stop.*
3. Do they expect to need it for more than 3 days? *Yes: stop.*
4. Are they breastfeeding? *Yes: stop.*
5. Have they ever been told they break down codeine unusually fast
   ("ultra-rapid metaboliser")? *Yes: stop.*
6. Do they have high blood pressure, heart disease, an overactive thyroid,
   glaucoma, difficulty passing urine, or COPD? *Yes: stop.*
7. Have they taken an MAOI antidepressant in the last 14 days? *Yes: stop.*
   Any other antidepressant or regular medicine? *Yes: review.*
8. Are they taking any other product containing paracetamol? *Yes: stop.*
9. Have they bought a codeine medicine in the last month? *Yes: review.*

## Set 4. Migraine: sumatriptan (1 product)

**Product:** Sumatran Relief 50mg 2Pk.

**Licence conditions** (SmPC PA0711/272/001):

- **Ages 18 to 65 only.** 100 mg in 24 hours at most.
- Migraine must have been diagnosed by a doctor, with **at least 5 attacks
  over at least a year**. Refer with 4 or more attacks a month, attacks lasting
  more than 24 hours, or a change in pattern.
- **A doctor or pharmacist must assess heart risk before supply.**
- Must not be used with: heart attack, heart disease or angina; stroke or
  mini-stroke; high blood pressure; irregular heartbeat; circulation problems
  in the legs; liver or kidney disease; seizures; sulphonamide allergy; an
  MAOI, ergotamine or another triptan; hemiplegic or basilar migraine.
- On the combined pill: refer if migraine began in the last 3 months, has
  worsened, or comes with aura. Take care with SSRIs, SNRIs and lithium.

**Draft questions:**

1. Is the person who will take it aged between 18 and 65? *No: stop.*
2. Has a doctor diagnosed their migraine? *No: stop.*
3. Have they had migraine for at least a year, with at least 5 attacks?
   *No: stop.*
4. Do they have 4 or more attacks a month, attacks lasting over 24 hours, or
   attacks that have changed in pattern? *Yes: stop.*
5. Have they ever had a heart attack, heart disease, angina, a stroke or
   mini-stroke, high blood pressure, an irregular heartbeat or poor
   circulation in the legs? *Yes: stop.*
6. Do they have liver or kidney disease, or epilepsy or seizures? *Yes: stop.*
7. Are they allergic to sulphonamide ("sulpha") medicines? *Yes: stop.*
8. Do they take an MAOI, ergotamine or another triptan? *Yes: stop.* An SSRI,
   SNRI or lithium? *Yes: review.*
9. Have they been told they have hemiplegic or basilar migraine? *Yes: stop.*
10. If they take the combined pill: did the migraine start in the last 3
    months, has it got worse, or does it come with aura? *Yes: stop.*

The licence asks for a heart-risk assessment by a doctor or pharmacist. These
questions do not replace it; your review of the order is where it happens.

## Groups we recommend handling without questions

You may disagree with either of these. Both would still have every order
reviewed by you; the difference is whether the customer answers questions
first.

### Pseudoephedrine decongestants (13 products)

**Products:** Actifed Tabs 12Pk; Advil Cold & Flu 20Pk; Benylin Dry Dual Action
100ml; Benylin Day & Night Tabs 16Pk; Lemsip Max Sinus & Flu Hot Lemon 10Pk;
Nurofen Cold & Flu 24Pk (two listings); Nurofen Sinus & Pain 24Pk; Sinutab
Non-Drowsy Tabs 15Pk; Sudafed Non-Drowsy Tabs 12Pk; Sudafed Syrup 100ml (please
confirm it contains pseudoephedrine); Sudaplus Non-Drowsy Tabs 15Pk; Zirtek
Plus Decongestant 6Pk.

**Licence conditions** (Non-Drowsy Sudafed SmPC PA23490/034/002; HPRA
register): age 12+, 5 days; must not be used with high blood pressure or heart
disease, beta-blockers, an MAOI in the last 14 days, diabetes, an overactive
thyroid, closed-angle glaucoma or severe kidney disease. The register limits
packs to 12 tablets and 720 mg, and appears to limit supply to one pack per
sale (the register text is cut off, so we could not confirm the wording).

**Why we suggest no questionnaire:** the main online risk with pseudoephedrine
is quantity, because it can be diverted, rather than suitability. One pack per
order, a check for repeat orders, and your review of every order address that
more directly than questions. **A short questionnaire is still defensible**
(blood pressure, heart disease, MAOIs, diabetes, thyroid). If you want one, it
becomes set 9 and the total rises to 39.

### The PSI's misuse categories: painkillers, antihistamines, laxatives (about 75 products)

**Products:** about 35 ibuprofen, paracetamol and aspirin products; about 16
non-drowsy antihistamines (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine); about 12
laxatives (Dulcolax, Senokot, lactulose, glycerol, Microlax, Fybogel); and 3
loperamide products (Imodium, Arret). Sedating antihistamines are in set 8,
and codeine is set 2.

**Why we suggest no questionnaire:** the PSI names these as liable to abuse or
misuse, but the measure it asks for is a system to spot repeated requests from
the same purchaser in a short time, not questions before each sale. A
questionnaire cannot tell that someone has bought the same painkiller three
times this week; a repeat-order check can. At licensed pack sizes the risk from
a single supply is low, and every order is reviewed by you in any case.
Putting questions on this group would take the total past 100 products.

**If you disagree,** the products we would start with are the stimulant
laxatives (Dulcolax: no more than 5 days daily use without investigation;
Senokot: more than a week needs medical supervision) and loperamide (see a
doctor if diarrhoea lasts more than 24 hours).
